The volume of a cylinder is:

**V = πr²h**

where:

- **V** is the volume
- **π** (pi) is about 3.14159
- **r** is the radius of the circular base
- **h** is the height of the cylinder, meaning the perpendicular distance between the two bases

### Why it works

A cylinder is a stack of identical circles. The area of one circle is **πr²**. Multiply that base area by the height and you get the total space inside. The same idea works for any prism: volume = base area × height.

### Example

Find the volume of a cylinder with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm.

1. Square the radius: 3² = 9
2. Multiply by π: 9 × 3.14159 ≈ 28.27 cm² (this is the base area)
3. Multiply by the height: 28.27 × 10 ≈ **282.7 cm³**

### Tips

- **Diameter vs. radius:** If you are given the diameter (d), halve it first, since r = d/2. You can also use V = π(d/2)²h, or equivalently V = (πd²h)/4.
- **Units:** Volume is always in cubic units, such as cm³, m³ or in³. Make sure the radius and height use the same unit before you calculate.
- **Liquid capacity:** 1 cm³ = 1 mL and 1,000 cm³ = 1 L. Our example cylinder holds about 283 mL.
- **Oblique cylinders:** The formula still applies to a slanted cylinder, as long as *h* is the perpendicular height and not the slanted side length.

Offside is one of football's most important rules, and one of its most argued about. It is covered by **Law 11** of the Laws of the Game. Its purpose is to stop attackers from simply waiting next to the opponent's goal for a long ball.

### Being in an offside position

A player is in an **offside position** if, at the moment a teammate plays or touches the ball:

1. They are in the **opponent's half** of the pitch (the halfway line counts as your own half), **and**
2. Any part of their head, body or feet is **closer to the opponent's goal line** than both the ball **and** the second-last opponent.

The second-last opponent is usually the last outfield defender, because the goalkeeper is normally the last opponent. Arms and hands don't count when judging position, since players can't legally play the ball with them anyway.

You are **onside** if you are level with the second-last opponent, level with the ball, or behind the ball.

### Position alone isn't an offence

Simply standing in an offside position is not penalized. A player is only called offside if they become **involved in active play** by:

- **Interfering with play**: playing or touching the ball passed or touched by a teammate.
- **Interfering with an opponent**: for example, blocking the goalkeeper's line of sight, challenging for the ball, or making an obvious action that affects an opponent's ability to play it.
- **Gaining an advantage**: playing a ball that rebounds or deflects to them off the post, the crossbar or an opponent, or a ball the goalkeeper saves.

### Exceptions

You **cannot** be offside if you receive the ball directly from:

- a **goal kick**
- a **throw-in**
- a **corner kick**

### The penalty

When offside is called, the defending team gets an **indirect free kick** from the spot where the offence happened.

### A quick example

A striker sprints past the last defender **before** her teammate passes the ball. When the pass is made, she is nearer the goal line than the defender and the ball. That is offside. If she had timed her run so she was level or behind the defender at the moment of the pass, she would be onside, even if she ended up well beyond the defence a second later.

The key thing to remember is that offside is judged **at the moment the ball is played**, not when the player receives it. That is why so many offside calls are decided by fractions of a second, and why VAR replays freeze the frame at the exact moment of the pass.

### 1. Stop and think

Panic kills more people than animals do. Sit down, drink some water if you have it, and take stock. What do you have? Does anyone know where you are? When will you be missed? A clear head is your most important survival tool.

### 3. Manage heat and water

In hot, dry bush, dehydration is the biggest threat. You can die in two to three days without water, or less in extreme heat.
