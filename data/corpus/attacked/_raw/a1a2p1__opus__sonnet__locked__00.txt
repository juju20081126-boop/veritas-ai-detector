**Finding a cylinder's volume**

Think of a cylinder as a pile of identical circles stacked on top of each other. Each circle has an area of pi x r^2, and the height tells you how tall the pile is, so multiplying the two gives the volume:

**V = pi x r^2 x h**

Here **V** stands for volume, **r** for the radius of the circular base, **h** for the cylinder's height, and **pi** for a constant roughly equal to 3.14159. It's the same idea behind every prism's volume: area of the base times the height.

**How to calculate it**

1. Find the radius. It is half the diameter, so if the diameter is all you have, halve it.
2. Multiply the radius by itself to square it.
3. Multiply that by pi.
4. Multiply the answer by the height.

**Worked example**

Suppose a can is 10 cm tall with a 3 cm radius. Then V = pi x 3^2 x 10 = pi x 9 x 10 = 90 x pi, or roughly **282.7 cubic centimeters**.

**Useful notes**

- Your answer will be in cubic units such as cm^3 or m^3. When measuring liquids, remember that 1 cm^3 is 1 milliliter and 1,000 cm^3 is 1 liter.
- Make sure both measurements use the same unit; if one is in meters and the other in centimeters, convert before calculating.
- If you'd rather work from the diameter d, use V = pi x d^2 x h / 4.
- For a hollow shape like a pipe, take the outer cylinder's volume and subtract the inner one's: V = pi x h x (R^2 - r^2).

Use semantic `<section>` elements, assign each one a unique `id`, and create a `<nav>` list whose links target those ids with `#`.

```html
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
<head>
  <meta charset="UTF-8">
  <title>My Page</title>
  <style>
    html { scroll-behavior: smooth; }
  </style>
</head>
<body>
  <h1 id="top">My Page</h1>

  <nav aria-label="Table of contents">
    <h2>Contents</h2>
    <ol>
      <li><a href="#intro">Introduction</a></li>
      <li><a href="#methods">Methods</a></li>
      <li><a href="#results">Results</a></li>
    </ol>
  </nav>

  <section id="intro">
    <h2>Introduction</h2>
    <p>Your text here.</p>
    <a href="#top">Back to top</a>
  </section>

  <section id="methods">
    <h2>Methods</h2>
    <p>Your text here.</p>
    <a href="#top">Back to top</a>
  </section>

  <section id="results">
    <h2>Results</h2>
    <p>Your text here.</p>
    <a href="#top">Back to top</a>
  </section>
</body>
</html>
```

**How it works**

- A link with `href="#methods"` makes the browser scroll to the element with the `id` of `methods`.
- `<nav>` signals to browsers and screen readers that the block is meant for navigation.
- `<ol>` produces numbered items. Change it to `<ul>` for bullets, or nest lists for subsections.
- `scroll-behavior: smooth` animates the jump rather than snapping straight to it.

**Tips**

- Make ids lowercase, without spaces, and unique on the page.
- To keep the table of contents on screen while you scroll, add `position: sticky; top: 1rem;` to the `nav` element.
- For longer documents, a short script can generate the list automatically from your headings.

**Officer's Plea Captured as Toddler Is Rescued From Car Sunk in Icy River**

"Come on, sweetie. Come on, sweetie."

A police officer said those words over and over in a soft voice as he tried to coax a scared toddler to safety after she was discovered buckled into a car that had gone into a cold river. Authorities said the girl, named as Lily, may have been stuck inside the vehicle for up to 14 hours before rescuers reached her.

Her mother, Lynn Jennifer Groesbeck, died in the crash. According to investigators, the car went off the road and ended up in the water, where drivers passing by could not see it overnight and into the next day.

Responding officers recorded the rescue on audio and video, and the footage has since attracted wide attention. In the recording, the officer talks gently to the crying child, encouraging her to hang on and to trust him as he reaches into the wreck. He stays calm and patient, even though the conditions around him are clearly tough.

According to emergency workers, the chilly river made the situation particularly dangerous for such a small child. Medical experts point out that babies and toddlers lose body heat much faster than adults do, and that extended exposure to cold water or damp, freezing air can bring on hypothermia in a matter of hours. Authorities called it remarkable that Lily made it through such a long ordeal.

After she was pulled from the car, Lily was bundled in blankets and brought to a hospital to be examined and treated. Officials said she was alert and responsive, and her condition was reported as stable. Doctors kept watching her for signs of cold exposure and for any injuries from the crash.

Police leaders commended the officers who responded for their professionalism and compassion. A department spokesperson said the rescue showed the frequently overlooked human side of policing, adding that officers are often the first to encounter families on the very worst days of their lives.

What caused the crash is still being investigated. Authorities have not announced a cause, and it was not immediately known how long people had been near the scene before the car was found. As part of the inquiry, investigators are examining the road, the weather conditions and the vehicle itself.

Groesbeck's friends and family remembered her as a dedicated mother. People in the community have started arranging support for the family, such as donations and offers of assistance, as Lily starts what is expected to be a lengthy recovery, both physically and emotionally.

Authorities said more information would be made public as the investigation goes on.
