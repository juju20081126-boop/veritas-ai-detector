Should Governments Ban Single-Use Plastic?

I think governments should ban single-use plastic—bags, straws, cups, all of it. We use this stuff once and it stays in the earth forever.

I see plastic bags all over my city, on the streets, in the river. It looks awful. And animals die from it. Fish and birds eat the plastic pieces and die. Then we eat the fish. So it comes back to us anyway.

People say a ban would be inconvenient. Yeah, maybe at first. Bringing your own bag feels annoying when you're not used to it. But it becomes a habit pretty fast. My family keeps cloth bags in the car now and barely think about it anymore.

Some plastic you probably can't get rid of—hospitals need it. But for regular stuff? Paper, glass, metal—they all work. So bans make sense to me.

Quantum mechanics predicts experimental results extremely well, but it doesn't tell you what's "really" happening. The main interpretations are:

- Copenhagen: The wavefunction is just a tool for predicting outcomes. Measurement "collapses" it, and asking what happens between measurements is often pointless.
- Many-Worlds (Everett): No collapse. Every possible outcome happens, and the universe branches into separate worlds.
- Pilot-Wave (de Broglie-Bohm): Particles always have definite positions, guided by a real wave. Deterministic but nonlocal.
- Objective Collapse (GRW, Penrose): Collapse is an actual physical process. This modifies the theory in testable ways.
- QBism: The wavefunction represents your personal beliefs about outcomes, not physical reality.
- Relational QM (Rovelli): Quantum states only exist relative to other systems. There's no observer-independent state.

They differ on realism, determinism, locality, and whether observers matter. Those are good themes for an assignment.

Amnesty Calls for Investigations Into Attacks on Afghan Women's Rights Activists

Amnesty International is pushing Afghan authorities to investigate attacks on women's rights activists, saying that people who threaten, assault, and kill them almost never get prosecuted.

Their report, "Their Lives on the Line," documents the persecution of women fighting for equal rights in Afghanistan. The danger comes not just from the Taliban and tribal warlords, but from government officials—the people who are supposed to protect them.

Activists told Amnesty about threatening calls, "night letters" telling them to stop, attacks on their families, bombings, shootings. Two heads of the women's affairs department in Laghman province were both killed in 2012. When activists reported threats to police, nothing happened. Sometimes the threats came from officials themselves.

Amnesty wants the government to investigate immediately and fairly, prosecute the people responsible, and actually protect activists at risk. They're also pushing international donors to keep funding women's rights work as foreign troops leave. Otherwise all the gains since the Taliban fell in 2001 could disappear fast.

But many activists keep going anyway. Despite knowing their lives are at risk, they run shelters, teach girls, provide legal help, and speak out publicly against abuses. Most of the time with almost no support.

Chilean Teenager With Cystic Fibrosis Asks President for Right to Die

SANTIAGO, Chile—A 14-year-old girl with cystic fibrosis asked President Michelle Bachelet directly for permission to end her life. This started a national argument about euthanasia in a country where assisted suicide is illegal.

Valentina Maureira posted a video from a hospital bed talking to the president. "I need to speak with you because I'm tired of living with this disease," she says. "I want you to authorize the injection that will make me sleep forever."

Cystic fibrosis clogs the lungs and digestive system with thick mucus, causing severe infections and organ damage. No cure exists. Treatment means daily physical therapy, medication, frequent hospital stays. It's exhausting. Her older brother died from it when he was young.

Her father said her request shocked them but the family would support her. She was worn out from repeated hospital admissions and invasive procedures.

The government responded quickly. Officials said the president couldn't grant the request—Chilean law doesn't permit euthanasia or assisted suicide. They said the government would provide all available medical and psychological support.

President Bachelet, a pediatrician herself, visited Valentina at the hospital. They didn't release details of the meeting.

The appeal reignited the argument in Congress over whether to legalize euthanasia under certain conditions. Supporters say patients with terminal, incurable illnesses should choose a dignified death. Opponents, including the Catholic Church, which still has influence in Chile, argue it would undermine protection of human life and could pressure vulnerable patients.

Medical ethicists point out the complexity because of her age. Even countries that permit euthanasia for minors—Belgium, the Netherlands—have strict rules about parental consent and whether the child can actually make that decision.

Valentina stayed in hospital receiving treatment.

Making friends as an adult usually means putting yourself in the same place as the same people over and over. Here's what actually works:

Join something that meets regularly. A running club, choir, soccer game, trivia night every week. That repetition turns strangers into friends.

Take a class. Pottery, improv, a language, rock climbing. You get a built-in topic to talk about and everyone else is new too.

Volunteer. Food bank, animal shelter, community garden. Working side by side builds connection fast and people already share your values.

Say yes to things. Go to the coworker's barbecue or the neighbor's party even when you want to stay home.

Be the one who invites. Most people wait for someone else to start. Suggest coffee, a walk, a game night. People say no sometimes but more will say yes than you'd expect.

Hit up old friends. Send that text to a former classmate or coworker. "Saw this and thought of you." Friendships come back.

Try the apps made for this. Bumble BFF, Meetup, local Discord servers connect you with people nearby who like the same stuff.

Follow up after hanging out. Most friendships end because nobody messages second. Send it.

Actually be interested. Ask questions, remember details, bring them up later. People know when you care.

Give it time. Real friendships take months of consistent contact.
