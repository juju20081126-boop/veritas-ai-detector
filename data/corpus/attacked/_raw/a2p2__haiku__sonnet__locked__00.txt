# The Flat Earth Movement

The Flat Earth organization promotes the concept that Earth is a disc rather than spherical. This organization is the most prominent modern association with flat-earth thinking, despite the worldview predating the organization itself.

## Origins

Samuel Birley Rowbotham, an English writer of the mid-1800s, asserted Earth lacks curvature based on Bedford Level experiments. Following supporters, including Lady Elizabeth Blount, expanded his arguments, creating the Universal Zetetic Society in 1893. Samuel Shenton established the contemporary Flat Earth organization in England in 1956. After Shenton's 1971 death, Charles K. Johnson in California took control, leading for years until fire destroyed records and he died in 2001. The organization resurged in the 2000s as an online presence.

## Adherent perspectives

The typical representation pictures Earth as a circular landmass with the North Pole positioned centrally and Antarctic forming the boundary ice. The sun and moon are considered compact and orbit above the disc. Participants typically deny authentic space imagery, asserting cover-ups by agencies and governments, and attribute phenomena like disappearing ship-hulls to optical properties or air effects.

## Established knowledge

Overwhelming evidence confirms Earth's spherical shape. Evidence includes differing star visibility at different latitudes, Earth's curved projection on the moon during lunar occultation, longitude differences, circumnavigation by aircraft and ships, orbital imaging, and measurements dating to Eratosthenes' two-thousand-year-old calculations. Mainstream science universally acknowledges this.

## Contemporary attention

From 2010 onward, flat-earth media proliferated on the web and video platforms. Gatherings have transpired, and certain personalities have backed the idea. Scholars researching conspiracy cultures highlight how the movement appeals to institutional skepticism more than geographic knowledge.

The group's sincerity remains debated, though members maintain commitment, and operations persist.
