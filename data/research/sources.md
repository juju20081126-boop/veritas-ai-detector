Access date for every web source below: 2026-10-01 (read via WebFetch; method/evaluation sections extracted with targeted prompts).
Tags: [documented] = stated in the source; [inferred] = our reading; numbers are quoted from the source.

## S1. Pangram 4 Technical Report — arXiv 2607.27183 — https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27183v1
- **Method [documented]:** open-weight MoE base + LoRA; shared backbone with heads for (a) 15-way segment AI-fraction, (b) token-wise 3-way (human / AI-assisted / AI-generated), (c) mixed-authorship binary, (d) humanizer detection (4-way probe, stop-gradient). Two-stage training; token-wise stage feeds the input twice ("Repeat2") so a causal model sees bidirectional context; CRF decoding + calibration + sliding 512-token windows (stride 256).
- **Data tactics [documented]:** human text openly licensed or company-owned; AI text generated in-house from the most-used LLMs; **synthetic mirroring** (extract topic from a human doc, regenerate on that topic); AI-assisted text built with the EditLens procedure; **active learning / hard negatives** (run on reserved human data, find human texts flagged as AI, mirror them, retrain). "Soft N-grams" labelling of edited text: lexical match -> human, semantic (embedding) match -> AI-assisted, neither -> AI-generated; f_AI = (0.5*C_AA + C_AG) / (C_H + C_AA + C_AG).
- **Evaluation [documented]:** 520,000 examples from 26 frontier models (Anthropic Claude Fable 5, Opus 5/4.8, Sonnet 5/4.6, Haiku 4.5; OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra, 5.5, 5.4, mini, OSS-120B; Gemini 3.1 Pro / 3.5 Flash, Gemma 4, Llama 3.3, DeepSeek V4, Mistral, Qwen, Grok, GLM ...) on open-ended Chatbot-Arena prompts; >1M human texts; ESL FPR measured on ELLIPSE, PELIC, ICNALE, TOEFL (24,586 texts).
- **Numbers [documented]:** FPR 0.0041% (95% CI 0.0032-0.0050%), FNR 0.3396%, AUROC 0.9916; FNR by family: Anthropic 0.195%, OpenAI 0.316%, Google 0.498%, DeepSeek 0.388%. Humanized text flagged AI 97.67% of the time; commercial humanizers 91.52-99.39%; **<50 words under the humanizer challenge TPR@1%FPR 73.32%** (full length 100%). ESL: 1 false positive in 24,586. AI-*polished* human text flagged AI only 0.01%; substantially AI-*edited* text recalled as mixed 55.01% (WildChat edits 65.17%); interleaved single AI/human sentences 75.02% token accuracy, 4-sentence chunks 92.41%.
- **Limits [documented]:** statistical; same text can score differently in different contexts; does not model people who write like LLMs; shorter text is harder.
- **Fit [inferred]:** the model is far beyond a CPU/INT8 budget, but the *recipe* transfers: synthetic mirrors (we have matched prompts), hard-negative mining loop, per-length reporting, separate "mixed authorship" handling, and a per-family FNR table. -> hypotheses H1, H2, H8, H12; report FNR per generator family.

## S2. ARB: Matched Authorship-Rewriting Benchmark — arXiv 2607.29539 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29539
- **Claim [documented]:** "AI-text detectors fail substantially on AI-rewritten human text", far worse than on fully AI-generated text. Matched pairs (human original <-> LLM rewrite: polish / paraphrase / rewrite) with three-way labels. CC BY 4.0 paper. (The extraction was partial: model list, sizes and per-detector numbers were not recoverable from the PDF text; treat details as [unknown].)
- **Fit [inferred]:** confirms that "human + AI-refined" is the hard boundary; matched human/rewrite pairs are exactly the structure of our `hybrid`/`human_ai_refined` slices. -> keep rewrites of *human* text as a separate evaluation slice, never as pooled negatives or positives.

## S3. PADBen — arXiv 2511.00416 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00416 (code: github.com/JonathanZha47/PadBen-Paraphrase-Attack-Benchmark)
- **Taxonomy [documented]:** iterative paraphrasing, sentence-level transformations, humanization, LLM-guided paraphrasing (Llama 3.1 8B, GPT-4, Gemini, rule-based). Detectors tested: DetectGPT, GPTZero, Binoculars, RADAR, HC3+, MAGE.
- **Finding [documented]:** paraphrase attacks severely degrade detection; **iterative paraphrase is the hardest**, and *intermediate* paraphrase states are where detectors break (statistics drift gradually while meaning is kept). Per-detector numbers were not recoverable from the extraction.
- **Fit [inferred]:** our A2 (iterative, 2-3 passes) family and the T3 target are well motivated; also supports training on intermediate states (pass-1 and pass-2 outputs both as positives).

## S4. Paraphrasing Attack Resilience of Various AI-Generated Text Detection Methods — arXiv 2605.14240 — https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14240
- **Setup [documented]:** Binoculars, fine-tuned RoBERTa (12k training texts), a 5-feature stylometric model (avg word length, lexical diversity, punctuation frequency, sentence-length SD, stopword ratio), and Random-Forest ensembles; attack = GPTinf paraphraser; 201 paraphrased AI + 201 human texts.
- **Numbers [documented]:** pre-attack F1: full ensemble 0.8061, text-features+Binoculars 0.8035, Binoculars alone 0.7497. F1 drop after attack: Binoculars 0.1964 (worst), RoBERTa+Binoculars 0.1879, text-features+Binoculars 0.1695, full ensemble 0.1345, RoBERTa alone 0.0992, text-features alone 0.0526 (most resilient). "Best-performing ensembles suffer the steepest losses" (performance/resilience trade-off).
- **Caveat [inferred]:** small n (402 texts), one paraphraser, one dataset.
- **Fit:** supports H4 caution (probability features degrade under paraphrase) and H5 (structure/stylometric features survive better); test on *our* paraphrase families with CIs before trusting.

## S5. Base Models Look Human To AI Detectors — arXiv 2605.19516 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19516
- **Claim [documented]:** detectors trained on instruction-tuned outputs miss text from *base* (non-RLHF) models; tested GPTZero, Pangram, DetectGPT on Llama 3 and Qwen 3 base vs instruct completions; base-model text is detected far less. Exact percentages were in figures (not recoverable).
- **Implication [documented]:** include unaligned base-model text in training. **Fit [inferred]:** add an attack family "base-model completion" generated locally with small base LMs (cheap on CPU) as a hard evasion slice; frontier models' RLHF signature is what our detector will mostly learn, so this is a real risk. -> candidate family A10 (to be added to schema if time allows).

## S6. Binoculars — Hans et al., ICML 2024 — arXiv 2401.12070 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.12070
- **Method [documented]:** score B = log PPL_M1(s) / log X-PPL_{M1,M2}(s): the observer's perplexity normalised by the cross-perplexity between two closely related models (Falcon-7B / Falcon-7B-Instruct family; the extraction garbled which is observer vs performer).
- **Numbers [documented]:** TPR > 90% at 0.01% FPR on ChatGPT text in news, creative writing and student essays; style-modified prompts ("Carl Sagan", "pirate") lower sensitivity by only ~1%; non-native English essays 99.67% accuracy on corrected and uncorrected sets (no ESL bias, unlike commercial detectors); famous memorised texts (US Constitution) can be flagged as machine-generated.
- **Limits [documented]:** no deliberate-evasion evaluation; no 30B+ models; black-box score without explanation; weak recall on low-resource languages.
- **Fit [inferred]:** two 7B models are impossible on our envelope, but the *ratio idea* (normalise by an expectation of the observer under a related model) can be approximated with a small base/instruct pair (e.g. Qwen2.5-0.5B pair). Counter-evidence S4: Binoculars lost 0.196 F1 under a GPTinf paraphrase, so the 1% "style prompt" robustness is not paraphrase robustness. -> H4: adopt only if it adds on paraphrased frontier text.

## S7. Fast-DetectGPT — Bao et al., ICLR 2024 — arXiv 2310.05130 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05130
- **Method [documented]:** conditional probability curvature (log p(x|context) - mean) / std under alternatives sampled from the model's own conditional distribution, computed analytically in one forward pass. White-box: same model samples and scores; black-box: surrogate (GPT-J sampler, Neo-2.7 scorer).
- **Numbers [documented]:** average AUROC 0.9615 (ChatGPT, three datasets) and 0.9061 (GPT-4); +75% relative over DetectGPT; 340x faster; black-box ChatGPT/GPT-4 "80% recall at 1% FPR"; **paraphrase attack AUROC 0.9641 -> 0.8715** (smallest relative drop among baselines); accuracy rises monotonically with passage length.
- **Limits [documented]:** needs token logits; surrogate models degrade; depends on model/corpus alignment.
- **Fit [inferred]:** small surrogate (<=1B) gives a usable but weaker feature; length dependence matches our bucketed reporting. -> H4.

## S8. RADAR — Hu et al., NeurIPS 2023 — arXiv 2307.03838 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.03838
- **Method [documented]:** adversarial game: a paraphraser (trained with PPO) rewrites AI text to evade the detector; the detector is trained on human text vs original + paraphrased AI text (reweighted logistic loss). 160K WebText docs; eval on XSum, SQuAD, WritingPrompts, TOEFL; 8 target LLMs (Pythia-2.8B, Dolly 1/2, Palmyra, Camel, GPT-J-6B, LLaMA-7B, Vicuna-7B).
- **Numbers [documented]:** AUROC 0.856 without paraphrase (best baseline log-rank 0.904); with unseen GPT-3.5-Turbo paraphrasing RADAR 0.857 vs best baseline 0.651 (+31.64%); with its own paraphraser 0.922; instruction-tuned detectors transfer better (Vicuna-7B -> LLaMA-7B 95.0%).
- **Limits [documented]:** weaker than the best baseline on un-paraphrased text (a real trade-off).
- **Fit [inferred]:** the training principle (train on original + paraphrased AI vs human) needs no GPU paraphraser if we pre-generate paraphrases (our A1-A5). The raw-text cost means we should track raw TPR and paraphrased TPR together. -> H2.

## S9. DIPPER paraphrase attack and retrieval defense — Krishna et al., NeurIPS 2023 — arXiv 2303.13408 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13408
- **Attack [documented]:** 11B paragraph-level paraphraser with lexical-diversity and sentence-reordering controls (0-100).
- **Numbers [documented]** (TPR at 1% FPR, open-ended 300-token generation, GPT2-XL, before -> after 60L/60O paraphrase): DetectGPT 70.3% -> 4.6%; watermark 100% -> 57.2%; OpenAI classifier 21.6% -> 14.8%; GPTZero 13.9% -> 1.2%.
- **Defense [documented]:** the API provider stores embeddings of everything it generated; flag text whose max similarity to the database exceeds a threshold: ~100% -> 96-98% after paraphrase at 1% FPR; needs ~5 TB/month at ChatGPT scale and only covers that provider's outputs.
- **Fit [inferred]:** retrieval needs a provider-side bank, which we do not have (H11 stays low value; we can only test a local bank of our own generations). DIPPER weights are 11B and not CPU-friendly; RAID already ships DIPPER-style paraphrases (attack `paraphrase`), which we use (A4_raid-t5).

## S10. Ghostbuster — Verma et al., NAACL 2024 — arXiv 2305.15047 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.15047
- **Method [documented]:** token probabilities from weaker models (unigram, trigram, GPT-3 ada/davinci) -> structured search over vector/scalar functions -> linear classifier.
- **Numbers [documented]:** F1 99.0 in-domain, 97.0 out-of-domain, 99.5 under prompt change, 92.2 on Claude text; beats GPTZero by 5.9 (in-domain) / 7.5 (OOD) F1; a commercial evasion tool cut recall from 99% to 62%; RoBERTa 98.1 in-domain but 88.3 OOD (domain overfitting); short non-native essays (91 TOEFL) F1 74.7; text <= 100 tokens degrades substantially.
- **Fit [inferred]:** (a) supervised encoders over-fit domains, so our domain-held-out evaluation and diverse training matter; (b) short text is a known weak spot, so headline numbers use >=150 words and short text is reported separately; (c) feature-search over probability sequences is feasible with a small LM.
## S11. GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers — Liang et al., Patterns 2023 — arXiv 2304.02819 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.02819
- **Setup [documented]:** 91 TOEFL essays (non-native, Chinese forum) vs 88 US 8th-grade essays (ASAP, native); seven detectors (Originality.AI, Quil.org, Sapling, OpenAI, Crossplag, GPTZero, ZeroGPT).
- **Numbers [documented]:** average 61.22% of TOEFL essays misclassified as AI vs ~5.19% of native essays; all seven flagged 18/91 (19.78%) unanimously. Cause offered: lower perplexity / less lexical variety. "Enhance word choices" with ChatGPT cut false positives 61.22% -> 11.77%; simplifying native essays raised their rate 5.19% -> 56.65%.
- **Fit [inferred]:** (a) perplexity-type features carry an ESL bias, so every candidate reports ESL FPR (W&I levels A/B/C, ~3,000 essays); (b) "simplicity" must not become a proxy for AI (confound checks in the brief); (c) note the study is 2023 and old detectors, but it is the standing worst case.

## S12. Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected? — Sadasivan et al. 2023 — arXiv 2303.11156 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11156
- **Theory [documented]:** best-possible AUROC is bounded by the total-variation distance between AI and human text distributions; paraphrasing and better models shrink that distance. TV is hard to estimate from finite samples (stated limitation).
- **Empirics [documented]:** soft watermark TPR@1%FPR 99.3% -> 9.7% after recursive paraphrasing; DetectGPT AUROC 96.5% -> 25.2%; fine-tuned RoBERTa-Large 100% -> 60%; 77% of paraphrased passages kept high content quality.
- **Fit [inferred]:** sets honest expectations: for heavy iterative paraphrase some loss is unavoidable, so T3 targets are stretch targets and the report must show where the floor is. Supervised detectors degraded least (consistent with S4/S17).

## S13. Pangram classifier technical report (2024) — Emi & Spero — arXiv 2402.14873 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.14873
- **Method [documented]:** transformer classifier trained on ~28M human documents (ten domains, datasets from 2021 or earlier); **hard-negative mining with synthetic mirrors**: train, generate AI "mirrors" matching human text in topic/length/style, find human false positives in the big pool, mirror those, retrain. Naive scaling saturated near 40,000 examples/domain; the loop cut false-positive rate 100x-1000x on holdouts.
- **Numbers [documented]:** 1,976 test documents, 8 LLMs, 10 domains: accuracy 99%, domain-weighted FPR 0.02%; ESL FPR 0% on TOEFL, 0% on ELLIPSE (3,907 essays), 0.09% on ICNALE (5,600); email held out of training: 0.8% FPR / 2% FNR; unseen open-source LLM recall > 99.6%; GPT-4o 100%, Claude 3 / LLaMA 3 99.76-99.97%.
- **Limits [documented]:** adversarial/paraphrase robustness "not stated"; student-writing domain gained little from mining (little data); authors discourage sole-arbiter use.
- **Fit [inferred]:** our matched prompts are a small-scale version of "mirrors"; ESL corpora ELLIPSE/ICNALE/PELIC/TOEFL are the right FPR sets but only W&I+LOCNESS is freely downloadable here (ICNALE/ELLIPSE need registration/Kaggle). -> H8.

## S14. RAID — Dugan et al., ACL 2024 — arXiv 2405.07940 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.07940
- **Composition [documented]:** 6.2M generations; 11 models (GPT-2, GPT-3, ChatGPT, GPT-4, Cohere, Mistral-7B, MPT-30B, LLaMA-2-70B, + chat variants), 8 domains, 4 decoding settings (greedy/sampling, repetition penalty 1.2 or not), 11 attacks (spelling variants, article deletion, homoglyph, whitespace, paraphrase, synonym, misspelling, number shuffle, zero-width space, case swap, paragraph breaks).
- **Protocol [documented]:** TPR at 5% FPR, justified as accuracy at a deployable false-positive budget.
- **Findings [documented]:** Binoculars strongest across models at low FPR; repetition penalty lowers accuracy by up to 32 points; sampling is harder than greedy; homoglyph and synonym attacks hurt most (-36 to -41 points for some detectors); accuracy occasionally *rises* after an attack; detectors are biased to training domains/models and "not yet robust enough for high-stakes use".
- **Fit [inferred]:** we already sample RAID (real attacks, 12 generators) for breadth; our headline is TPR@1% FPR (stricter than RAID's 5%), and we also report TPR@5% FPR so numbers are comparable to the RAID literature. Homoglyph/zero-width are solved by input normalisation (H9).

## S15. TH-Bench — arXiv 2503.08708 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.08708
- **Setup [documented]:** attacks: paraphrasing (Dipper, Recursion, Prompt), perturbation (RAFT, HMGC), data mixing (TOBLEND); 13 detectors (metric-based: log-likelihood, rank, log-rank, entropy, GLTR, LRR, Binoculars, Fast-DetectGPT; model-based: RADAR, OpenAI, ChatGPT detector, ConDA, LM detector); three axes: evasion, text quality, compute.
- **Findings [documented]:** "no single attack excels on all three axes" (impossibility triangle). Recursion degrades quality badly (perplexity +41.4 on Essay, cosine similarity < 0.65); Prompt and RAFT preserve quality (RAFT cosine > 0.95).
- **Fit [inferred]:** justifies our quality gate (sentence-embedding cosine >= 0.80 + fluency) before counting an attacked text, and justifies LLM-prompt paraphrase/humanizer families (A1-A3) as the realistic, quality-preserving threat.

## S16. DAMAGE — Detecting Adversarially Modified AI Generated Text — arXiv 2501.03437 (ACL GenAIDetect 2025) — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2501.03437
- **Setup [documented]:** 19 humanizer tools tested (DIPPER, Grammarly, QuillBot, BypassGPT, Ghost AI, HIX Bypass, Humbot, HumanizeAI.io/.pro, Humanizer.com, Phrasly, Semihuman, StealthGPT, StealthWriter, Surfer SEO, Undetectable AI, Twixify, WriteHuman). Detector: Mistral-NeMo 12B + LoRA; data from GPT-3.5/4, Claude, LLaMA, Mistral, Gemini; humanizer outputs were only ~0.68% of the final data, oversampled 18x; active learning with hard negatives.
- **Numbers [documented]** (TPR at 5% FPR): raw AI 100.0%, humanized 98.26%, RAID paraphrase 93.0%, RAID synonym 97.0%, detector-optimised humanizer 93.2%. Competitors on raw/humanized: GPTZero 99.73%/60.04%, Binoculars 94.15%/28.23%, RADAR 3.33%/5.05%.
- **Limits [documented]:** focused on the hardest ("L1") humanizers; humanizer data limited by subscriptions; evaluated mainly on student essays.
- **Fit [inferred]:** the key lesson is data efficiency: a *small* amount of real humanizer output, oversampled, taught robustness. This supports (a) our LLM-humanizer family A3, (b) asking the user for a small hand-run batch of commercial humanizer outputs (A8), (c) oversampling attacked samples in training. -> H2.

## S17. Stumbling Blocks — stress-testing MGT detectors under attacks — arXiv 2402.11638 — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.11638
- **Setup [documented]:** 4 attack families — editing (typos, homoglyphs, format characters), paraphrasing (synonym, span, sentence/paragraph), prompting (prompt paraphrase, in-context learning, character-substituted generation), co-generating (emoji/typo insertion during decoding); 8 detectors: metric-based (GLTR, Rank, LogRank, DetectGPT), fine-tuned (OpenAI, SimpleAI, DeBERTa), watermark.
- **Findings [documented]:** average performance drop 35% across attacks; 2-6 typos push metric-based detectors below random; co-generating drives some to ~0.1 AUROC; model-based detectors are more robust than metric-based ones; combine both.
- **Fit [inferred]:** motivates normalisation + trained (model-based) detectors as the backbone with probability features as optional extras (H4/H10), and a typo-injection family (A7).
