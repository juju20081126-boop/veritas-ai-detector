# Attack catalogue (test-data tooling only; never exposed through backend/ or the UI)

Purpose: build a *realistic* evaluation and training set of AI text that someone has tried to hide. Families are fixed in advance and are never tuned against a specific commercial detector (rule 2.2). Every attacked text records `generator_id` (the model that wrote the ORIGINAL), `attack_id` (`<family>_<variant>`), `parent_id`, and the tool in `access_path`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** LLM paraphrase | Rewrite the text with an LLM that is *not* the author | Claude Code subagent (`claude-code-subagent:<alias>`): Sonnet paraphrases Opus text and Opus paraphrases Sonnet text; Haiku 4.5 for cheaper passes | `A1_light` (change some wording, keep structure), `A1_medium` (rewrite most sentences), `A1_heavy` (restructure paragraphs, change sentence order where meaning allows) | >= 40 (strengths cycled 14/13/13) | S4 (GPTinf), S9 (DIPPER), S12; paraphrase is the most effective evasion (PADBen, S3) |

## Quality gate (every LLM/local attack)
Keep an attacked text only if (a) sentence-embedding cosine similarity to its parent >= 0.80 (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), (b) word count within 0.6x-1.6x of the parent, (c) it is not identical to the parent (5-gram Jaccard < 0.9) and not a refusal/meta text. Rejected counts are reported per family (TH-Bench, S15: some attacks wreck quality; those would not be used by a real attacker).

## Human-side controls
The benign attacks (A5 back-translation, A7 typos/zero-width/homoglyph at the same rates, light A1 "polish") are also applied to a sample of *human* texts so that "has been transformed" is not itself a label. Their rows keep `label=human`, `attack_id=<family>`, `parent_id=<human text id>`; they are evaluated as a separate "attacked human" slice and count toward the realized-FPR report.
