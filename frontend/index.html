How to read the marks

After you check a text, each sentence in the document is marked in one of four ways.

  • Plain ink Human-written. Natural variation in rhythm and word choice.
  • Teal underline Human, AI-refined. A human draft polished by an AI tool.
  • Amber highlight AI, then paraphrased. AI text run through a rewriter or humanizer.
  • Pink highlight AI-generated. Reads as direct output from a language model.

Detection is a statistical estimate, not proof. When the signals are borderline, Veritas says so instead of guessing.

Method & limits

Documented limits from peer-reviewed literature (see README Limitations for S-numbers):

  • Non-native English false positives: Non-native English writers can be wrongly flagged as AI. Liang et al. (Patterns 2023, S11) found 7 detectors averaged 61.22% false positives on 91 TOEFL essays vs ~5.19% on native essays due to lower perplexity and simpler vocabulary.
  • Short text is unreliable: Texts under 80 words lack reliable statistical signatures (Ghostbuster S10 degrades on ≤100 tokens; Fast-DetectGPT S7 accuracy scales with length; Pangram S1 drops to 73.32% under 50 words). 150+ words is recommended.
  • Paraphrasing and humanizers: Paraphrased/humanized text is the hardest case. In DAMAGE (S16), detection of humanized text fell to 60.04% for GPTZero and 28.23% for Binoculars. Iterative paraphrasing (PADBen S3) breaks statistical detectors.
  • Vendor figures are self-reported: Commercial accuracy claims (e.g. Pangram S1/S13) are self-reported. Independent RAID benchmarks (S14) show detectors are domain-biased and not robust enough for high-stakes sole-arbiter use.
  • Signals, not proof of authorship: A score is a probabilistic signal, not proof of authorship (Pangram S1/S13). Memorized texts like the US Constitution can be flagged as machine-generated (Binoculars S6).

Veritas runs entirely on this computer. Results are probabilities, so use them as one input alongside your own judgment.
